Wind → melt → ice

A week on the prairie, then the envelope.

This page is not a remix of the Home notes. It follows weather through the house, then scores that weather against the three materials Facebook lists. Still photographs are atmospheric plates — not Always job evidence.

Wind

The course that lifts.

Prairie wind finds a loose shingle or a corner that was already tired. What to say: address, which slope faces the weather, whether anything is on the lawn. Repair if the roof can be saved. Replace if it cannot. Always does not publish a brand list — ask Mike what is on the truck.

Melt

Water after the thaw.

A stain on a ceiling after melt is a path, not a mystery. The path may start at a ridge, a flashing, or an eave that could not shed. Say whether the interior is wet and which rooms. Service area is Regina & area — name the neighbourhood.

Ice

The eave holds or it dumps.

Ice at the drip after a thaw-freeze is often a gutter and edge problem. Water that should have left the house sits, then finds the wall. Named service: gutters. If siding is taking the splash, say the wall is wet too.
