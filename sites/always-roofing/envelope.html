Regina week

Wind, then melt, then ice — through the roof, the wall, and the trough.

Prairie weather is why most Regina roofs get called. This page is homeowner education for the three trades Facebook lists: roofing, siding, gutters. Photographs are atmospheric plates, not job evidence. Always does not publish a brand list, a storm-claim desk, or a drone package — ask Mike what he will actually do on your house.

Wind

Shingles and corners after a prairie blow.

Open prairie wind finds a loose shingle, a tired ridge, or a corner that was already lifting. After a blow, walk the yard before you walk the roof. What to say: address, which slope faces the weather, whether anything is on the lawn, whether the interior is dry.

Repair if the roof can be saved. Replace if it cannot. Mike will tell you which — this page will not guess a square count or a shingle brand. If an insurer is involved, say that on the first call.

Melt

A ceiling stain after a chinook or a hard thaw.

A stain on a ceiling after melt is a path, not a mystery. Water that sat as ice overnight finds a ridge, a flashing, or an eave that could not shed. The stain often shows two rooms over from the entry. Say whether the interior is wet, which rooms, and whether it is still dripping.

A chinook or a March thaw on a Regina roof is ordinary. Waiting for the next freeze makes the path worse. Service area is Regina & area — name the neighbourhood.

Ice

Ice at the drip, water on the wall.

Ice at the drip after a thaw-freeze is often a gutter and edge problem. Water that should have left the house sits, then finds the wall or the soffit. Named service: gutters. If siding is taking the splash, say the wall is wet too.

Do not chip ice off an eave from a ladder in January unless you already know the roof. Call and describe the drip. Hours are not published — ask when Mike can come.

Wall

Siding after rain or a lifted course.

Siding is on the same public list as roofing. Call when a course lifts, a corner is tired, or water sits at the wall after rain. Say which elevation and whether the interior is stained. Ask what they will hang — vinyl, fibre-cement, and stucco packages are not printed here.

Before you call Mike

Should I get on the roof?

Not in ice, and not if you do not already work roofs. Photograph the yard debris, the stain, and the elevation from the ground. That is enough to start 306-209-5007.

Repair or replace?

Ask. Isolated missing shingles are often a repair. A tired field, a failed deck, or a leak that has moved rooms is a different conversation. Mike will say which. This page will not push a replacement you have not asked for.

Insurance?

If you have a claim number, say so. Always has not published an adjuster packet or a financing plan. He will tell you what he actually does.
