A week on the prairie, then the envelope.

This page is not a remix of the Edition classifieds. It follows weather through the house, then names the three materials Facebook lists: roof, siding, gutters. Still photographs here are atmospheric plates — not Always job evidence. Confirm the work on the call.

First · Wind

The course that lifts.

Prairie wind finds a loose shingle or a corner that was already tired. What to say: address, which slope faces the weather, whether anything is on the lawn. Repair if the roof can be saved. Replace if it cannot. Always does not publish a brand list — ask Mike what is on the truck.

Next · Melt

Water after the thaw.

A stain on a ceiling after melt is a path, not a mystery. The path may start at a ridge, a flashing, or an eave that could not shed. Say whether the interior is wet and which rooms. Service area is Regina & area — name the neighbourhood.

Then · Ice

The eave holds or it dumps.

Ice at the drip after a thaw-freeze is often a gutter and edge problem. Water that should have left the house sits, then finds the wall. Named service: gutters. If siding is taking the splash, say the wall is wet too.

Materials on the public list

Facebook names three trades. That is the book. The plates on this page are not paired as proof that Always installed a particular product. Ask what they will use on your house.

Shingle / roof

The top of the envelope. Wind lifts a course. Melt shows a stain. A roof you already know is finished still needs an address and a look.

On the call: slope, leak or no leak, storm or age.

Cladding / wall

Siding sits with roofing because the wall and the roof are one envelope. A lifted course or water behind a corner after rain is a wall call, even if the ridge looks fine.

On the call: which elevation, water behind the skin or not.

Trough / gutter

Gutters and the drip edge move water off the house. Fail them and the wall and roof edge take the hit. Ice dams are often that path.

On the call: sheeting on the wall, ice at the eave, stain under the drip.
